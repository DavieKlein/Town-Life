# Town Life - Story Overview (one page)

Status: writing proposal for the owner. Progression (upgrades, secrets, free play, status screen, skill gates): `docs/progression.md`. Details: `docs/story_chapter1.md` (incl. section 3B), `docs/story_chapter2.md`,
`docs/story_chapters3-5.md`. Own characters only; light and funny, with serious moments.

## The arc in one paragraph
{NAME} is fired on day one when billionaire **Victor Vault** buys Corner Market and turns it into the cold **VaultMart**. With $20 and a park
bench, {NAME} slowly builds a life: a dish job, odd jobs, Old Block's neighbours, a steady job on the Docks, then a business, a team, a
community. Each of the five types (Grafters, Makers, Techies, Ballers, Posh) teaches a craft and becomes an ally. Vault's "Smart City"
(robots, self-checkouts, a city brain called AURUM, the demolition of Old Block) grows meaner every chapter, until the allies, an
old co-op charter and Vault's own recorded words undo it. Rich, in the end, means who you share the table with.

## Rival (Dex) arc
Ch.1 five small beats (sorry look, tired smile, the certificate he does not report, silence at the bridge, "No idea.") -> Ch.2 Regional
Manager, brings AutoPort, quietly sabotages the race ("sensor dust"), eats Rosa's bread -> Ch.3 Smile Index, a real smile, learns he is a
"placeholder" -> Ch.4 replaced by a robot manager and fired (turning point) -> Ch.5 three paths by `dex_decency` and friendship: redeemed
(joins the chain, works at Quill & Solder), grey (small shop), cold (leaves; a postcard).

## Villain (Vault) arc
Buys the shop -> closes the bridge -> digs the VaultLink cable trench (AURUM Trunk 7) at Old Block -> void demolition notice -> AutoPort -> DataVault -> VaultPass/HealthPods -> machine-signed notice #2
-> buys the football club and a council vote -> the Gala and the AURUM Switch. Downfall: the 1931 Co-op Charter makes the sale void; the
Co-op tokens (Ch.1 collectibles) are the proof; Vault's own recording ("People are furniture") plays at the Gala; Valet, who cannot lie,
testifies; the hearing voids the notice; the human chain stops the digger; at the end his gold card fails at a self-checkout and Rosa
gives him a shift stocking shelves at the warm Corner Market. Tower becomes a public library.

## Themes
1. **Poor to rich = who you become:** every step up asks for help given or taken (the lent room, the co-op tokens).
2. **Warm human city vs. cold automation:** palettes turn warm area by area; machines are not evil, Vault's use of them is (Nadia, Valet).
3. **Community as property:** the Co-op Charter, the penthouse that nobody locks.
4. **Rivals and truce:** Grafters/Posh and Ballers/Techies mentors share a table (M49, M59) and the half-speed rule ends.

## Systems in the story
Needs and sickness (Okafor, the faint), Social (parties, Repair Café), friendship + City Coins (the co-op token "pay later"), gifts and
gossip (each chapter adds rules), phone texts (one nudge per mission), collectibles (coins = Co-op lore, postcards = Mia's family, flyers =
resistance), events (Saturday market, march, Block Party, Repair Café, Lantern Night, Silent Protest, Cup Match, Gala, Hearing).

## Owner decisions on the ten open questions (s66)
1. **Lent space = Ines's laundromat back room: YES.**
2. **Co-op Hall / penthouse: kept as planned.** What is the Co-op Hall? In 1931 the people of Old Block (bakers, dockers, seamstresses, a bridge crew) founded a
   cooperative and built a shared hall: a market hall with stalls on the ground floor, a meeting room and flats above, owned by nobody and everybody under the
   Co-op Charter. Over the decades it fell into disrepair and now stands as the empty building on the demolition site that Victor Vault wants to clear for glass
   towers. At the end of the story the hall is rebuilt as a market hall (stalls, flats) and its top floor becomes the **penthouse**, the last step of the housing
   ladder: open, shared, a door without a lock, a key on a hook, a long table with mismatched chairs. It echoes Vault's golden tower, but is the opposite.

## Owner decisions, s68

11. **Why Old Block is closed at first (option B): the VaultLink cable works.** VaultTech is "laying underground hyperspeed cables" (public name **VaultLink HyperFibre**,
    "The future, four metres down"; in truth **AURUM Trunk 7**, the nerve line from the golden tower to a hatch called NODE 7 under the demolition site). Orange barriers, a trench, cable drums,
    the sign "SURVEY IN PROGRESS - NO ENTRY", the work robot TRENCH-E 3 and the bored guard Pruitt block the arch from the start, so the player sees the closed route.
    **Opening (owner: sneak route):** in M09 Mia shows **a bent-open hole in the VaultTech fence and a one-cell path between overgrown bushes** behind the barriers (squeeze animation, leaves rustle,
    "You didn't see me") that leads into Old Block. After the meeting (M13) the neighbours move the barriers aside, hang laundry on them and Teo lays his door across the trench as "Teo's Bridge"
    (the front entrance is open for good). The trench foreshadows Vault's plan: Pier 4 cable (M22), Nadia explains it (Ch3), the cup-match blackout is a Trunk 7 test pulse (M52), the Gala key wakes
    Trunk 7 (M65). Full scene, lines, map note and art list: `docs/story_chapter1.md` section 3B.2a.
12. **No fixed days.** The story is never tied to calendar days; Day 1 (the prologue) is the only fixed day. Missions are driven by triggers: a previous mission, a flag, a phone text, money, a number
    of shifts, "the next morning after X", and natural times of day (shop hours, a meeting at 15:00, night scenes). Weekly events (Saturday market, Repair Cafe) are optional recurring world events and
    staged on request when a story beat needs them. All tables in the four story documents now use "Starts when" triggers instead of day numbers; newspaper headlines carry triggers, not days.
13. **Demolition Countdown ("Afteltijd") rule.** Notice #1 (Ch1, void) has no counter. **Notice #2 (M42) starts "Demolition in 30 days".** The number drops by 1 at 6:00 every in-game day but never below the
    **floor of the current stage**; stages are set by story beats (A: M42 cap 30 floor 20 -> B: M52 cap 20 floor 12 -> C: M56 cap 12 floor 6 -> D: M61 cap 6 floor 3 -> E: M64 cap 2 floor 2), and each stage
    sets the counter to the smaller of its value and the cap. A slow player simply rests at the floor (Valet: "Rescheduled. Awaiting protagonist."); nothing bad happens. The only way to 0 is the
    scripted finale chain: Gala (starts when the player says "I'm ready" to Odette, 20:00, counter 2) -> Hearing the next morning 9:00 (counter 1) -> Demolition Morning the morning after at 7:00 (counter 0, the human chain).
    Each step requires the previous story step to be won, so 0 can never come early and the player can never lose. The counter disappears after the downfall. Details: `docs/story_chapters3-5.md` section 0.
14. **Progression design (docs/progression.md):** one upgrade ladder (skills with ranks I-V, 13 tiered City Coin perks with caps, type-star passives, temporary buffs), 12 secrets (including the sewer
    sweet seller Mothball at 00:00, whose cheap sweets give buffs and whose expensive sweets, $40 rising to $150, give permanent skill points up to a cap), free play after the credits (exclusive shops, vintage
    vehicles, Legend titles, the penthouse as showcase), a pause-menu Status screen (reputation stars, skills, People list, perks, Doors) and skill gates (e.g. Charm for a club, Logic for a lab) that never block the critical path.

## Owner decisions, s69

15. **The time machine "The Late Tram" (main-story finale of Chapter 4, M54a-M54e; was a side arc until s70, see decision 16, `docs/story_chapters3-5.md` section 4.6).** Built by Pip (12) and Nadia in the **Quarter Lab** (Station Quarter, Logic 25, soft gate), a retired tram ticket booth with a dryer-drum door, powered by a
    battery "Quarter Cell" and Nadia's dormant AURUM forecast core. Three trips: (1) **the Forecast**, AURUM's draft of Smart City Year 20 (cold, Old Block gone, bench meters, the old Valet in a museum case, a lonely Vault behind a door, the older player as a
    Heritage Greeter with one warm copper token), seen once; (2) an **accident in ancient Egypt** (a noodle jams the year dial: 45 becomes 4,500), where Pharaoh Menkhet takes the player for a minor god; (3) the real target, **Rosa's Corner Market 45 years ago**, where the
    player watches Rosa fire 13-year-old Vic Waldt for taking a loaf for his little sister, and the draught that slams the door is the player (the past is not changed, only visited). Afterwards the core is cut out and the machine goes **only to Egypt**, one trip a day for a
    Cell: deben (the yard's money), the Menkhet furniture set, collectibles, Tomb Honey for Mothball, a growing pyramid and a 13th secret, the Sealed Annex (`docs/progression.md` section 2.2). The arc supplies required beats in M62, M66 and M69
    and explains the fifth Co-op coin behind the Corner Market sign (Vic's payment). All art NEEDS PROPOSAL.
