# Reference study: Sims games on handhelds (inspiration only)

Purpose: learn from the *genre* (The Sims Bustin' Out GBA, The Urbz GBA/DS, The Sims 2 GBA/DS). Nothing here is to be copied: no characters,
names, graphics, music, exact layouts. No images or assets were downloaded.

**Evidence quality warning.** In the research session only web *search* worked; page fetching was blocked (gamespot, wikipedia, cubed3,
nintendoworldreport did not resolve). So section 1-3 rest on search-result snippets of reviews plus general background knowledge of the games.
Items marked (U) are unverified from sources and come from memory; check them with gameplay videos before leaning on them.

## 1. What the sources say (verified from review snippets)

Sources:
- Bustin' Out GBA: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sims_Bustin%27_Out), [Worthplaying](https://www.worthplaying.com/article/2003/12/30/reviews/14446-gba-review-the-sims-bustin-out/), [Maximum Utmost](https://maxutmost.com/review-the-sims-bustin-out-gba/), [GameFAQs reviews](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/gba/917846-the-sims-bustin-out/reviews), [Rigelatin](https://rigelatin.net/staircase/sims-bustin-out.php), [DarkZero](https://darkzero.co.uk/game-reviews/the-sims-bustin-out-gba/)
- Urbz GBA/DS: [Cubed3 GBA](https://cubed3.com/review/88/1/the-urbz-sims-in-the-city-game-boy-advance.html), [Cubed3 DS](https://cubed3.com/review/246/1/the-urbz-sims-in-the-city-nintendo-ds.html), [GameFAQs reviews](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/gba/920434-the-urbz-sims-in-the-city/reviews), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Urbz:_Sims_in_the_City), [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/the-urbz-sims-in-the-city-review/1900-6113880/)
- Sims 2 handhelds: [Cubed3 GBA](https://cubed3.com/review/400/1/the-sims-2-game-boy-advance.html), [Nintendo World Report DS](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/4442), [Worthplaying GBA](https://worthplaying.com/article/2005/12/11/reviews/29600-gba-review-the-sims-2/)

**Movement/camera.** Handheld Sims switched from the PC "hand of god" cursor to *direct control of one character with the D-pad*; reviewers compare
it to an Animal Crossing / RPG-sim hybrid. Urbz controls are "simple but sometimes wonky; the Sim rushes off somewhere" (Game Informer via
GameFAQs/GameSpot). Camera can be zoomed in close in Urbz (GameFAQs reader).

**Look.** Mixed: GamePro "slightly sloppy" (Bustin' Out), others "charming style that captures the franchise despite hardware limits";
Urbz characters "drawn more realistically than before". Sims 2 GBA was called "less a simulation, more a platformer" (that is the version to avoid copying).

**UI.** Social interaction through a dialogue/menu system; on conversation the camera zooms in on the two characters and the player picks from a
menu (Wikipedia-derived). Sims 2 GBA: cursor-based menus that work on GBA buttons. Sims 2 DS collapsed needs into a single meter (NWR).

**Systems.** Needs, skills with progress bars, friends, optional home decorating; jobs as minigames; Bustin' Out has a mission mode plus Freeplay;
open neighbourhood with a dozen+ unlockable locations; motive drain gentler than on PC. Urbz: nine districts, "Rep" per district/group, categories
that gate which minigames you may play and at what level; Urbz minigames include matching puzzle, window washing, stand-up comedy, motocross (GameSpot).

**Praise.** Direct control makes it feel like a real handheld game; variety of minigames; unlockable places that give a sense of progress;
Urbz "a distinctive, surprisingly solid game", GBA Urbz 72 Metacritic, Bustin' Out GBA 79.
**Criticism.** Repetition ("similarity of events day in, day out", Cubed3); needs chores "about as much fun as it sounds" (GameSpot); depth
cut versus PC; wonky pathing/control; Sims 2 GBA dull and platformer-like.

## 2. Background knowledge (U, unverified here)

- Characters walk in 8 directions on an isometric 2:1 grid, short walk cycles, tiny idle animations; interactions open a short pop-up list next to
  the object (Sims-style "pie" became a vertical list on handhelds); object use plays a looping action animation with need bars rising.
- Conversations: portrait + text, a relationship meter, a short list of social actions (greet, joke, compliment, ...), which unlock deeper
  actions as the relationship rises.
- Music: per-location looping tracks, a distinct jingle for conversation/minigame; Sims "simlish" voice babble is a signature.
- Houses: pre-built lots, objects bought from catalogue; building mostly cut on GBA.

## 3. Town Life vs. the genre

Already matching or ahead: direct D-pad control (8 facings, run), pre-rendered 3D iso look with day/night, per-pixel depth, timed activities with
animation and gradual bars, quality levels on fixtures, NPC portraits with friendship meter and small talk topics (likes/dislikes/mood), minigames
(STOCK IT!, PLATE RUSH), missions + log, save, phone, collectibles, clinic/sickness, a story with a villain (stronger than the sources' loose
mission modes), warm/cold city idea (original).

## 4. Prioritised, original improvements for district 1

Effort: S = a day or less, M = a few days, L = a week+. All visual items need a proposal first (owner's rule).

1. **Repetition guard for jobs (S/M):** the most repeated complaint. Vary PLATE RUSH with daily "specials" (different tempo, rush types, a regular's
   order), tips tied to friendship, and 2-3 odd jobs (notice board) so shifts are not identical. Matches the roadmap odd jobs item.
2. **Make needs rewarding, not a chore (S):** you already give bonuses when needs are high; add a visible "Feeling great" state (small pay/skill
   bonus, brief icon) and keep decay gentle (reviewers liked gentler drain). Avoid a single collapsed meter.
3. **Unlockable places as visible progress (M):** keep the blocked routes (Old Block cable works, closed bridge); add a "places discovered" list in
   the pause menu/Collection with stamps. Cheap, strong sense of progress.
4. **Sneak-route and shortcut feel (M):** the M09 sneak route is an original hook; add a few small hidden shortcuts and secrets in district 1.
5. **Control polish (S):** reviewers criticised wonky auto-pathing; you use direct control, so keep it; add tiny turn-and-stop animations (a short
   braking frame, a head look toward the interactable on A-cue). Matches "NPC walk cycles" backlog item.
6. **Idle and ambient animations (M, proposal):** player idle variations after N seconds (stretch, check phone, look around); NPCs with 2-3 idle
   loops and walk cycles (already on the backlog). Biggest "alive" gain per effort.
7. **Conversation camera feel (S/M):** a subtle push-in or letterbox during talks and a short "reaction" bubble/emote over heads (original icons:
   heart, sweat, note) when friendship changes; layouts 1/2 already exist.
8. **Relationship tiers with unlocked actions (M):** at friendship thresholds (e.g. 20/45/70) unlock new small-talk topics, a favour, a gift tip, a
   shared activity (sit together on a bench). Builds on the existing meter and City Coins at 70.
9. **Location audio identity (L):** per-area loops (market warm, VaultMart cold muzak, park, diner) plus a short conversation/minigame stinger and
   own non-verbal "mumble" blips per character; follow docs/music_plan.md. Original compositions only.
10. **Home personalisation, small scope (M):** the housing ladder plus catalog already exists in design; a handful of buyable pieces for the first
    back room gives the decorating hook without a build mode.
11. **Interaction menu clarity (S):** keep the short list; show need effects as small icons next to each option (+hunger, +fun) so choices read
    at a glance. Check "Gifts" and shop menus do the same.
12. **Avoid:** collapsing needs into one meter, platformer-style segments in a sim, long unskippable chores, hidden rules for gated minigames
    (always show why a job is locked, as you do for districts).
